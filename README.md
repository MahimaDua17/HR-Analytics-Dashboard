The dashboard presents a detailed overview of employee attrition across various dimensions including age, gender, education, job role, salary, and years of experience. The data covers 1,470 employees, out of which 237 have left the organization, resulting in an attrition rate of 16.1%. The average age of employees is 37, the average salary is 6.5K, and the average years of service is 7 years.
🔍 Key Findings:
1. Attrition by Gender:
Male attrition: 150

Female attrition: 87
👉 Male employees have a higher attrition rate compared to females.

2. Attrition by Age Group:
Highest attrition is in the 26–35 age group (116 employees).

Followed by 18–25 age group (44 employees). 👉 Young professionals (18–35 years) are the most likely to leave.

3. Attrition by Education:
Life Sciences (38%) and Medical (27%) are the most affected education backgrounds. 👉 Employees from Life Sciences and Medical fields show higher attrition.

4. Attrition by Job Role:
Laboratory Technician (62) has the highest attrition.

Followed by Sales Executive (57) and Research Scientist (47). 👉 Technical and sales-related roles are experiencing higher turnover.

5. Attrition by Salary Slab:
Majority of attrition is from employees earning up to 5k (163 employees).

Attrition significantly drops as salary increases. 👉 Lower salary is a major driver of attrition.

6. Attrition by Years at Company:
Peak attrition at 2 years (59 employees).

A noticeable number also leave at 5 and 10 years. 👉 Employees tend to leave early in their tenure or during milestone years.

7. Attrition by Department (Top Filter):
Affects Human Resources, Research & Development, and Sales. 👉 These departments should be the focus of retention strategies.

✅ Recommendations:
Review compensation policies, especially for those earning under 5k.

Strengthen employee engagement and career development programs for early-career professionals (under 3 years).

Conduct exit interviews to understand reasons for high attrition in technical and sales roles.

Focus on retention in the 26–35 age group, as they form the largest segment leaving the organization.
